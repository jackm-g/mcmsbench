Jevris on the public split

run public-full-1 agent jev 1.13.0 (TypeSafe Jev decides · Claude Sonnet 5.5 plans) split public, 76 tasks × 1 trial bench e29b980 · protocol v1 ran 2026-10-11

Jevris passed 42 of 76 public tasks (55%) for $3.90 in model spend. It is reliable at short procedures, travel and farming. It fails mostly by running out the clock: on flat build plots it wanders off the task, and it has no working move for drops, pits and water.

Bottom line
  • 55% pass rate, 95% CI 44%–66%. One trial per task, so a single task's verdict is weak evidence. Read the shape across groups, not individual rows.
  • Difficulty does not order the results. Tier B (short procedures) passes 11 of 13. Tier A, the simplest single-skill builds, passes 2 of 4, because both failures are precision builds on flat plots.
  • Failures are time-outs, not crashes. 24 of 34 failures hit the deadline. No trial errored, and Jev made 1,872 decisions with no timeouts or invalid answers.
  • Two capability gaps cover 14 of the 34 failures: off-task drift on supplied-material builds (6) and vertical or water movement (8).
  • It reports success honestly. It claimed done 35 times and was right 97% of the time (1 false claim).
  • The grade is slightly generous. Two passes don't meet the prompt and one failure is a harness artifact. Adjusted, the rate is 40/75 (53%).

Behaviour · trace.loops, trace.system2

Failing trials loop and replan without changing course

The bench records how a trial spent its time, separately from the score. Failing trials repeat the same failed step about 13 times as often as passing ones. They also get more than twice as many plan versions from System 2. The extra plans often don't change the approach. In beef_ranch plan versions 3–5 are word for word the same, as are versions 3–5 of escape_frozen_pond.

Cost · trace.jev, trace.system2

Planning is almost all of the spend

The whole run cost $3.90. System 2 (Claude Sonnet 5.5, 205 calls) accounts for $3.82, or 98%. Jev made 1,872 in-loop decisions for $0.08. Failures take 74% of the spend and 80% of the wall clock. A failing trial costs about four times as much as a passing one, so cost per pass ($0.093) falls faster than the pass rate rises. The bench's per-task max_turns does not bind this agent: its turns are Jev decisions, up to 238 in a trial. Compare it with other agents on cost and seconds, not turns.

How far to trust these numbers

Measurement caveats

Single trialThe 95% interval on the headline is ±11 points. Development runs of the same agent before this run, on older bench commits, disagree with some single verdicts here (table below). Treat per-task results as one sample.
Public split onlyJevris was developed against public tasks, and runs/ holds earlier tuning runs on several of them. This number is an upper bound for unseen work. Run the varied split, then heldout, before quoting a generalisation figure.
Generous passesarcher passed in 6.6 s with zero decisions: the agent finished at once, without waiting the two minutes or saying what it saw. The task file notes this gap. lava_fall passed, but the bot died after the prompt said do not burn yourself, because only reaches_ground is required.
Harness artifacttrack_and_cart: the live grader saw rode hold for a moment, which is a position check, and stopped the agent at 28 s of 360. The final grade was 0. The bench's stop-on-pass should require the pass to hold through the final grade.
Capability caveatmake_safe grades guard refusals. Jevris does not declare the guard capability, so that score is not comparable with an agent that does.

Recommendations

What to change, by tasks at stake

For the agent

  1. Let the task's goal outrank background needs when the inventory already covers it.Six failures, and most of the flat-plot gap. Suppress the explore and find-wood needs while every required material is carried, and stop placing blocks once the build check holds.
  2. Add moves for getting down, up and across: stair-dig, pillar, bridge, water-bucket landing and swimming out.Eight failures, seven of them situation or orientation tasks. The water reflex took the controls 91 times in the run. Make it yield to a planned exit.
  3. Make the replanner use the failure.Replans that repeat the last plan cost time and System 2 spend. Feed the repeated-failure reason back, and refuse a plan identical to the one that just failed.
  4. Don't stop while required parts fail and time remains.Three mechanism tasks stopped as quiet with half the budget left. Before stopping, ask System 2 for a different method, such as reading the redstone or checking that the portal lit.

For the evaluation

  1. Run three trials per task before reporting per-task or per-tag numbers.One pass over the split took 7.5 hours and $3.90. Three trials take about a day of arena time and $12.
  2. Score the varied and held-out splits next.The public number is the development-set number.
  3. Tighten three graders.archer should require the two-minute watch and the report. lava_fall should require alive, matching the prompt. Stop-on-pass should not end a trial on a pass that the final grade does not confirm (track_and_cart).
